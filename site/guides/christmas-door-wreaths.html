Door wreaths

A door wreath is the classic round ring of greenery and trimmings that welcomes guests at the door. These picks range from a natural-looking twig wreath with pinecones and red berries to full pine wreaths with bows, bells, a "Merry Christmas" sign and string lights. They come from independent shops, so sizes and what's included vary; check each listing before you order.

  1. Measure your door first and pick a wreath about a third to half its width so it looks balanced.
  2. Use an over-the-door hanger or a removable hook instead of nails, and check your building rules on decorating unit doors.
  3. Keep wreaths away from candles and heat, and switch off any lights at bedtime or when you head out.

Door garlands

Swags and garlands are the longer cousins of the wreath. A teardrop swag hangs top-down on a door or rail like a bouquet, while a garland is a long rope of pine you drape around the door frame, a console or a stair rail. Pair either one with a wreath for a fuller, layered entrance.

  1. Drape a garland around the door frame with removable hooks, then hang a wreath or swag in the centre for a layered look.
  2. Check that a garland won't block the door's swing, the peephole or the door number.
  3. If the lights use batteries, keep spares handy and take them out when you pack the decor away.

Hang it right

Four tips for a welcoming Christmas door

Size the wreath to your door

Measure your door's width before you order. A wreath that covers roughly a third to half of the door's width looks balanced; too small and it gets lost, too big and it can catch when the door swings. Listings often show sizes in inches, so convert from your tape measure (12 inches is about 30 cm, 16 inches about 40 cm).

Common-sense advice

Hang it without nails

Most condo buildings don't allow nails or drilling on unit doors, so check your building rules first. An over-the-door wreath hanger or a removable adhesive hook is the usual nail-free choice. Make sure the hanger is thin enough for the door to close fully and won't scratch the paint.

Common-sense advice

Layer a garland around the frame

For a fuller, more styled entrance, drape a pine garland around the door frame and hang a wreath or swag in the centre. Repeating the same accents, such as pinecones and red berries, on both pieces ties the look together.

Source: Homes & Gardens: Christmas wreath ideas

Use lights safely

Check that lights are in good condition before hanging them, keep them away from anything that gets hot, and switch them off when you go to bed or leave home. Battery-powered lights on a wreath avoid a cord running across the doorway.
