The 5 phone accessories worth owning, ranked

Built from six expert lists, then matched to real Lazada listings: a flight-friendly power bank, a fast charger, a cable that lasts, commute-ready earbuds and a tripod for trips.

Updated Oct 12, 20269 min read5 picksPrices checked Oct 12, 2026

Heads up: links to Lazada on this page are affiliate links, so we may earn a small commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you. This guide was researched with the help of AI tools. Here's how we pick.

Your phone carries a lot of your day: payments, rides, work chats, the family group chat, maps and every photo from the last out-of-town trip. The accessories around it don't need to be fancy. They need to keep it charged, steady and easy to use, and they need to be genuine, because a fake charger or power bank isn't just money wasted. It can overheat.

So instead of starting from whatever sells, we started from the experts. We read six editorial accessory lists, counted what they agree on, set clear criteria for each item, and only then compared real listings against them.

How we built this list

Six expert lists, one shortlist

We read six editorial lists: Engadget, 9to5Google, SamMobile, CNET, How-To Geek and Unbox PH. The power bank is the only accessory all six include. Next come a case and a spare charging cable (five of six), then a wall charger and a stand, grip or mount (four of six).

We ranked the slots by how many lists include them, how often you would use them, and how much they matter here: long commutes, brownouts, flights and heat. Cases and car mounts are covered in the notes below instead of as picks.

What we did

  1. Read six editorial accessory lists and counted which accessory types they share.
  2. Ranked the types by agreement, daily use and Philippine relevance, then set 3 to 4 checkable criteria per slot.
  3. Compared 2 to 4 Lazada listings per slot on those criteria, price, seller and recent buyers.
  4. Checked every product photo against its listing, and every power bank model code against Anker's 2025 recall list.
  5. Dropped look-alike stores (we found fake "Anker" shops spelled with Greek and Cyrillic letters) and listings whose photos didn't match.

Flying soon? Know the power bank rules

CAAP bans power banks over 160Wh on flights, and up to 100Wh is allowed on board. Philippine Airlines and Cebu Pacific allow a maximum of two power banks of 100Wh or less. They must travel with you, not in checked bags, and both airlines say to keep them out of the overhead bin and not to charge with them during the flight. A 10,000mAh power bank is about 37Wh. Check your airline's latest rules before you fly.

Sources: Unbox PH, CAPA, Cebu Pacific Help

Always check the model number

In June 2025, Anker recalled five power bank models (A1257, A1647, A1652, A1681 and A1689) because of battery cells from one supplier. Our pick, the A1685, is not one of them, and while researching we skipped a listing whose name still carries a recalled code. Whatever power bank you buy, make sure the model on the box matches the listing.

Sources: 9to5Mac

Protect it first: case and screen protector

Cases and screen protectors are on most expert lists, but they have to match your exact phone model, so there is no single pick for everyone. Some phones sold here come with a basic clear case and a pre-applied screen film, so check the box first. If you buy one, choose the listing made for your model, ideally from the brand's own store.

Sources: SamMobile, 9to5Google

Why there is no car mount (yet)

Car mounts appear on three of the six lists, but none of the mounts we checked had an independent review we could verify, so we are holding off rather than guessing. If you use one, mount it low on the dashboard or an aircon vent, never in the middle of the windshield: the Anti-Distracted Driving Act (RA 10913) bans placements that block your view of the road.

Sources: DriveSafe PH
