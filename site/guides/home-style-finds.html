MUJI-inspired minimalism

MUJI is a Japanese retailer known for plain, practical basics with no visible branding. A MUJI-inspired home borrows that idea: storage first, quiet colours, and everyday things that look good because they're simple. It suits condo living well, because a tidy small room always feels bigger.

  1. Hide clutter first. Matching drawers and clear bins do more for a small room than any decor piece.
  2. Keep to two or three colours, like white, oat and light wood, and repeat them around the room.
  3. Choose soft, clean scents and plain containers over decorative ones.

These aren't MUJI products, and Great Finds isn't affiliated with MUJI. They're similar finds for the same calm look.

Japandi

Japandi blends Japanese minimalism with Scandinavian comfort: low, simple furniture, natural wood, soft neutral colours and handmade-looking textures. It's as calm as a minimalist room, but warmer and more lived-in.

  1. Pick one wood tone and repeat it, in a tray, a cabinet or a stool.
  2. Use muted, earthy colours like oat, clay, sage and charcoal instead of bright accents.
  3. Leave some walls and surfaces empty. The breathing room is part of the look.
  4. A round paper lantern lamp is a classic finishing touch. We haven't found one that passes our checks yet, so it isn't linked here.

Modern Filipino

Modern Filipino style brings local natural materials like rattan, abaca, buri and capiz into a clean, modern room. Think woven textures, warm wood and soft, glowing light, without the clutter. It feels right at home in the tropics and celebrates materials many of us grew up with.

  1. Mix one or two woven pieces with plain, modern furniture so the room feels fresh, not like a souvenir shop.
  2. Keep rattan, abaca and buri in well-ventilated spots and wipe them with a dry cloth; natural fibres can grow mould in humid rooms.
  3. Use LED bulbs in capiz lamps. They use less energy and give off less heat than old incandescent bulbs.
