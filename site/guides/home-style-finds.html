Find your home style: MUJI-inspired, Japandi and Modern Filipino finds

Three calm, condo-friendly looks built from honest materials (glass, wood, stoneware, cotton and native fibres) as small sets you can actually buy.

Updated Oct 25, 202610 min read9 picksPrices checked Oct 25, 2026

Heads up: links to Lazada on this page are affiliate links, so we may earn a small commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you. This guide was researched with the help of AI tools. Here's how we pick.

Your home doesn't need a renovation to feel like you. Most of a style comes down to a handful of choices: the colours you repeat, the materials you can touch, and how much you leave out.

For each of the three looks below, we read how design writers define it, picked the few pieces that do the most to create it in a small space, and only then searched for products that fit. Each set shares one palette, so the pieces look right together.

How we built this list

Define the look, then choose honest materials

Design writers agree on what carries each look: Livingetc and Homes & Gardens point to neutral bases, natural materials (wood, cotton, linen, ceramics) and a few functional, well-made pieces, while Tatler Asia and CITEM name narra, capiz, abaca, buri and rattan for modern Filipino rooms. We also set one material rule: no plastic. Each style now has four different product types in glass, bamboo, wood, stoneware, cotton or native fibres, sharing one palette so a single room can hold all four.

What we did

  1. Read two to four design and craft sources per style and noted the palette, materials and signature pieces they agree on.
  2. Chose four slots per style that create the look in a small space, with criteria for material, colour and size.
  3. Compared 2 to 4 Lazada listings per slot, favouring named brands, local makers and established stores.
  4. Checked every product photo to make sure the item really fits the look and matches its listing.
  5. Kept each set to one palette so all the pieces work in a single room.

MUJI-inspired minimalism

A calm, storage-first look rooted in Japanese ideas of simplicity, with fewer things that are each useful, in soft whites, beiges, greys and pale wood with cotton, linen and other natural textures. We use MUJI only as inspiration: none of these picks are MUJI products.

soft whitecream / oatclear glasspale bamboomuted sage
  1. Declutter first, then buy. Livingetc's designer advice is to 'declutter, and then declutter again', and to keep storage subtle, seamless and tidy.
  2. Choose wood, cotton, linen and other organic textures, as designer Yoko Kloeden advises in Livingetc. Clear glass and matte stoneware keep the same quiet feel.
  3. Repeat one colour and material: one bedding shade, matching jars, one plate set. A full shelf then reads as one calm block.

MUJI is named here only as the inspiration. These aren't MUJI products, and Great Finds isn't affiliated with MUJI.

References: Livingetc · Dwell

Japandi

Japandi blends Japanese minimalism with Scandinavian simplicity: clean lines, natural materials and genuine craftsmanship, warmer and more textured than stark minimalism. Expect warm whites and light beiges, wood, linen, handmade-looking ceramics and soft, diffused light, with most clutter behind closed doors.

warm whiteoat / soft beigemushroom taupewarm oaksand stonewarecharcoal accent
  1. Try the 20/80 rule from Homes & Gardens: about 20% of your things in sight and 80% behind closed doors.
  2. Keep the base to warm whites and light beiges, and bring colour in only through small accessories like a lampshade or throw pillows (Homes & Gardens).
  3. Favour functional pieces in natural materials (Livingetc's examples: ceramic cups, a teapot, books) and add one living plant.

References: Livingetc · Homes & Gardens · Homes & Gardens

Style 3 of 3

Modern Filipino

Modern Filipino style brings the country's own natural materials, such as narra and other hardwoods, capiz shell, abaca, buri and rattan, into clean, contemporary rooms instead of heritage-heavy ones. Keep walls and big pieces neutral, then let one glowing capiz light and a few woven and wooden accents carry the look.

whitebeige / taupehoney acaciaburi and abaca tancapiz pearlleafy green
  1. Use local natural materials such as capiz, abaca, buri, rattan and acacia or narra as accents, not everywhere. Tatler Asia's Hurray Design homes pair them with contemporary finishes.
  2. Keep the base neutral (white, beige, taupe) so the woven textures and the capiz glow stand out.
  3. In a humid condo, give woven pieces air. Don't push baskets flat against walls or leave damp laundry in them.

References: Tatler Asia · CITEM · The Philippine Star · Wikipedia
