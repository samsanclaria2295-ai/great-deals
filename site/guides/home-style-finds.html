Find your home style: MUJI-inspired, Japandi and Modern Filipino finds

Three calm, condo-friendly looks, each defined by design writers and built as a four-piece set you can actually buy, from clear storage that tames clutter to a capiz lamp that glows like home.

Updated Oct 12, 202610 min read12 picksPrices checked Oct 12, 2026

Heads up: links to Lazada on this page are affiliate links, so we may earn a small commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you. This guide was researched with the help of AI tools. Here's how we pick.

Your home doesn't need a renovation to feel like you. Most of a style comes down to a handful of choices: the colours you repeat, the materials you can touch, and how much you leave out.

For each of the three looks below, we read how design writers define it, picked the four pieces that do the most to create it in a small space, and only then searched for products that fit. Each set shares one palette, so the pieces look right together.

How we built this list

Define the look first, then shop it

A style isn't a random pile of nice things. So for each look we started from design sources, wrote down its palette, materials and signature pieces, and built a four-piece shopping list around them.

Where the perfect piece wasn't available from a seller we trust, we say so and explain the substitute, like a linen drum lamp standing in for a paper lantern.

What we did

  1. Read two to four design and craft sources per style and noted the palette, materials and signature pieces they agree on.
  2. Chose four slots per style that create the look in a small space, with criteria for material, colour and size.
  3. Compared 2 to 4 Lazada listings per slot, favouring named brands, local makers and established stores.
  4. Checked every product photo to make sure the item really fits the look and matches its listing.
  5. Kept each set to one palette so all four pieces work in a single room.

MUJI-inspired minimalism

A calm, storage-first look rooted in Japanese ideas of simplicity and function. Every item should earn its place, and most things live out of sight. Design writers describe it as a neutral palette of soft whites, beiges, greys and pale wood, with natural textures like cotton and linen and simple, fuss-free forms. The MUJI brand itself is known for minimalist, restrained design. We use it only as inspiration: none of these picks are MUJI products.

soft whitegreigepale greyclear / translucentpale wood
  1. Declutter first, then buy storage. Livingetc's designer advice: keep only what you use and love, and make storage subtle, seamless and tidy.
  2. Skip stark pure white. Layer off-whites, soft greys and pale wood so the room feels calm, not cold (Livingetc).
  3. Buy storage in one colour and shape, so a full shelf reads as one quiet block instead of many small things.

MUJI is named here only as the inspiration. These aren't MUJI products, and Great Finds isn't affiliated with MUJI.

Style references: Livingetc · Dwell

The shopping list

MUJI-inspired rooms start with storage, so two of the four pieces fight clutter: lidded boxes for closet shelves, and clear bins for the pantry, fridge or vanity. Plain cotton bedding in a soft neutral calms the biggest surface in a condo bedroom, and a glass reed diffuser adds a quiet scent with no cords or blinking lights. Everything stays in white, clear and soft beige, so the four read as one calm set.

Style 2 of 3

Japandi

Japandi blends Japanese minimalism with Scandinavian simplicity: clean lines, natural materials and real craftsmanship, but warmer and more textured than stark minimalism. Expect a neutral base of warm whites and soft beiges, wood in both light and darker tones, linen, handmade ceramics and soft, diffused light. Hanging rice-paper lights are the classic example. Most clutter is kept behind closed doors.

warm whitesoft beige / oatmushroom taupewarm oak brownsand stonewarecharcoal accent
  1. Try the 20/80 rule from Homes & Gardens: about 20% of your things on show and 80% behind closed doors.
  2. Use soft beige instead of bright white as your base, and bring colour in only through small things like a lampshade or a cushion (Homes & Gardens).
  3. Buy fewer, better-made pieces in natural materials, and add one living plant. Both Livingetc and Homes & Gardens treat greenery as part of the look.

Style references: Livingetc · Homes & Gardens · Homes & Gardens

The shopping list

The sources agree on four signatures: soft, diffused light, wood, handmade-looking ceramics and natural textiles, with clutter tucked away. So this set is a warm wood-look drawer cabinet for closed storage that doubles as a bedside table, a linen-shaded lamp on a wood base for the glow, a ribbed stoneware cup and saucer for the ceramic, and a plain mushroom-taupe cushion for soft texture. They share oat, sand, taupe and warm oak tones, so they sit naturally together on one bedside or reading corner. Honest gaps: no rice-paper lantern and no stoneware vase in the affiliate feed passed our checks, so the lamp has a linen-look shade and the ceramic is a cup set.

Style 3 of 3

Modern Filipino

Modern Filipino style puts the country's own natural materials into clean, contemporary rooms instead of heritage-heavy ones: narra and other hardwoods, capiz shell, abaca, buri and rattan. Philippine design is known for natural, endemic materials worked by hand, and woven abaca and rattan pieces read as relaxed accents in modern homes. Keep walls and big pieces neutral, and let one glowing capiz light and a few woven and wooden accents carry the look.

whitebeige / taupehoney acacia woodnatural buri and abaca tancapiz pearlleafy green
  1. Use local natural materials such as capiz, abaca, buri, rattan and acacia or narra as accents, not everywhere. The Hurray Design project in Tatler Asia pairs them with contemporary finishes.
  2. Keep the base neutral (Tatler Asia's example uses grey, white, brown, taupe and beige) so the woven textures and the capiz glow stand out.
  3. In a humid condo, give woven pieces air. Don't push baskets flat against walls or leave damp laundry in them (common-sense care).

Style references: Tatler Asia · CITEM · The Philippine Star · Wikipedia

The shopping list

The look rests on a few local materials in a clean room, so each slot carries one of them: capiz for light, buri for storage, rattan and abaca for the table, and acacia for wood. A small capiz lamp gives the pearly glow the material is prized for. A lidded buri hamper hides laundry in something handsome enough to keep in the sala or bedroom. A rattan-and-abaca basket makes merienda or fruit look intentional. A round acacia tray brings warm local wood to the table. All four share honey, tan and pearl tones against white walls, so they work together in one condo dining-and-living corner. Three of the four come from small Filipino craft sellers, and we say below where their recent sales are low.
