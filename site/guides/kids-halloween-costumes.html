Kids' Halloween costumes 2026: superhero, anime, spooky and cute

36 kids' costumes in four moods: superhero, cartoon and anime, spooky-but-fun and cuddly-cute, picked for mall trick-or-treats and school costume days before October 31.

Heads up: links to Lazada on this page are affiliate links, so we may earn a small commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you. This guide was researched with the help of AI tools. Here's how we pick.

Halloween falls on a Saturday this year, 31 October 2026, but for many kids the costume comes out sooner. Last year, malls and hotels around Metro Manila held kids' trick-or-treat parties and costume contests in the last weeks of October, and school costume days often land in the same stretch. Ordering now leaves time for delivery and a try-on at home before the big day.

We've grouped the picks into four looks so you can jump straight to what your child loves: superhero for little heroes, cartoon and anime for fans of their favourite shows, spooky for kids who like a friendly fright, and cute for toddlers and anyone who'd rather be adorable than scary. Check each pick's size chart before you add it to your cart.

How we built this list

Well-reviewed, kid-friendly and ready for October 31

We picked these costumes from Lazada listings with strong buyer feedback, and the featured picks come from different sellers, so you're not relying on one shop. We leaned toward comfy, kid-friendly costumes over gory ones. Most character costumes here are from independent shops, not official merchandise, and we say so on each one.

What we did

  1. Split the list into four moods kids ask for: superhero, cartoon and anime, spooky-but-fun and cute.
  2. Shortlisted costume listings with strong buyer feedback, and chose featured picks from different sellers.
  3. Checked every product against Lazada's affiliate feed for price and stock on the day we published.
  4. Noted sizes, what's included and when a character costume is from an independent shop rather than official merchandise.
Shopping list based on: American Academy of Pediatrics, HealthyChildren.org: Halloween Safety Tips (updated 2026) · US Consumer Product Safety Commission: Halloween Safety: Tips for Selecting Costumes and Decor · US Food and Drug Administration: Halloween Safety Tips: Costumes, Candy, and Colored Contact Lenses (2023)

Measure first, then check the chart

Age ranges on listings are only a starting point, since sizes vary from shop to shop. Measure your child's height, chest and waist, then compare with the seller's size chart. Between sizes? Go up one, but make sure the hem won't drag on the floor.

Superhero costumes

Superhero looks are an easy win for school costume day and mall trick-or-treats: kids already know the pose, and a mask or cape does half the work. These are independent-shop costumes, not official merchandise, picked to cover padded muscle suits, a Pinoy comics heroine and simple caped sets for every budget.

  1. Masks can block side vision, so push the mask up while walking or swap it for face paint; test the paint on a small patch of skin first.
  2. Look for "flame resistant" on the label, and keep capes and long skirts away from candles and lit jack-o'-lanterns.
  3. Dark suits are hard to see at night, so add a strip of reflective tape or carry a glow stick for evening trick-or-treats.

Spooky costumes

Classic spooky looks are the easiest Halloween costumes to pull off: a cape, a pointed hat or a skeleton print, and your child is ready for the school parade or mall trick-or-treat. These picks stay on the scary-fun side, and most come in a wide range of sizes so siblings, and even parents, can match.

  1. For walking around, choose face paint or a mask worn up on the head; full masks can block your child's view (CPSC, AAP).
  2. Capes and robes should clear the floor, so pin up long hems before stairs and crowded mall corridors.
  3. Dark costumes are hard to see after sunset and loose fabric can catch fire, so add reflective tape and use battery lights, not candles.

Before trick-or-treat

Four tips for a happy, safe Halloween

Check the fit, then order early

Costumes and shoes should fit well, and hems should be short enough that your child won't trip or snag on things. Order early so there's time for a try-on at home before the event.

Source: American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org)

Look for a flame-resistant label

Check costume, mask and wig labels for "flame resistant". Skip big baggy sleeves and long capes around candles, and use flameless or battery lights in jack-o'-lanterns.

Source: US Consumer Product Safety Commission

Keep their view clear

Masks can block a child's view, so face paint and a hat make a good swap. Make sure hats can't slide over the eyes, and add bright colors or reflective tape for walking after dark.

Source: American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org)

Patch-test face paint first

Test face paint at least 24 to 48 hours before the event by dabbing a little on your child's arm. If redness, a rash or swelling shows up there, it may be an allergy, so don't use it.

Source: US Food and Drug Administration
