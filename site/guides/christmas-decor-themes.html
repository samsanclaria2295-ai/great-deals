Six Christmas decor themes, and the pieces to pull each one off

Gold, champagne, classic red and green, white, candy-colourful or proudly Filipino: pick a theme, then shop the four pieces that build it in a small condo corner.

Updated Oct 12, 202612 min read24 picksPrices checked Oct 12, 2026

Heads up: links to Lazada on this page are affiliate links, so we may earn a small commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you. This guide was researched with the help of AI tools. Here's how we pick.

The easiest way to make Christmas decor look intentional is to choose a theme and repeat it. A short list of colours and materials, used again and again, makes even a tiny sala look planned rather than busy.

Below are six themes, each built as one small-condo corner of four pieces you can buy now, in good materials like glass, wood, ceramic, velvet, felt and handwoven textiles wherever we could find them.

How we built this list

One colour story per corner

Design editors agree a Christmas scheme should start with a short colour list and repeat it: gold works alone or softened with white or blush, red and green look richest in deep berry and forest shades with a metallic accent, and one colour in mixed finishes looks more expensive than many colours. We built each theme as one small-condo corner of four different pieces, favouring glass, wood, ceramic, velvet, felt and handwoven textiles over plain plastic.

What we did

  1. Read design editors on Christmas colour schemes and noted what makes each palette work.
  2. Planned each theme as one small-condo corner with four different kinds of piece: a tree, ornaments, light or greenery, and a textile or centrepiece.
  3. Compared Lazada listings per slot, favouring glass, wood, ceramic and handwoven pieces over plain plastic.
  4. Checked every product photo and variant for colour fit and for religious imagery, and kept lights to USB, battery, solar or certified options.

Theme 1 of 6

Gold Christmas

A gold Christmas works because one warm metal, repeated in shiny, matte and glitter finishes, looks rich and calm instead of busy. Keep the tree's green as the only other colour and light it all in warm white, and it reads more modern than red and green.

Champagne goldAntique goldWarm whitePine greenCream
  1. Mix finishes, not colours: shiny, matte and glitter gold side by side look richer than one finish.
  2. Pile gold balls in a clear bowl on the sala table; it glimmers without blocking anyone's view.
  3. Use warm-white lights only. Cool white makes gold look grey and flat.

Theme 2 of 6

Champagne and blush Christmas

Champagne and blush pairs soft pink with pale, pinkish gold, so the metal warms the pink and the pink softens the shine. Snowy flocking, clear glass and a handwoven texture keep it grown-up and cosy rather than sugary.

Blush pinkChampagneRose goldSnow whiteDusty rose
  1. Let the tree carry the pink and keep ornaments in rose gold and champagne, so the corner reads soft, not candy.
  2. Mix shiny, matte and glitter balls of one colour for depth without adding more colours.
  3. Add one natural texture, like a handwoven runner, so the pastels feel lived-in.

Theme 6 of 6

Filipino Christmas (capiz and parols)

A Filipino Christmas corner decorates with the country's own materials: a glowing capiz parol, woven rattan or abaca, and handwoven cloth on the Noche Buena table. Keep the base neutral so the shell's glow and the fibres' texture do the decorating.

capiz pearl whiteparol reddeep greennatural rattan taninabel cream and rustwarm white light
  1. Hang the parol on the wall, not the floor: Federal Land suggests a parol on the wall as a condo space-saver, and it frames the whole corner.
  2. Use two or three native materials and repeat them. CITEM describes Philippine design as natural, endemic materials like abaca, buri, bamboo and rattan in earth tones, so let capiz, rattan and handwoven cloth carry the look.
  3. Plug-in capiz pieces need a PS mark or ICC sticker, one socket each (no 'octopus' wiring), and switching off at bedtime (Meralco, BFP).
